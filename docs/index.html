Material: lithium

Fusion needs lithium. Nobody can trace it yet.

Fusion power plants run on a fuel called tritium, which is almost impossible to find in nature. So the plants will have to make their own, using a special kind of lithium. This site follows that lithium from the ground to the power plant, and shows who could get hurt along the way.

How to read this site

Every fact links to where it came from. When people accuse a company of something, we say who made the claim. A trade record shows two companies did business, but not what was in the box. When we don't know something, we say so. Not knowing doesn't mean a company is clean or guilty.

Words to know

Lithium-6
A rare type of lithium. Only about 7 out of every 100 lithium atoms are this kind. Fusion plants need much more of it than that.
Tritium
The fuel fusion plants burn. Plants make it by hitting lithium-6 with tiny particles called neutrons.
Enrichment
Sorting lithium to collect more of the lithium-6 kind. Until now this has mostly been done to build nuclear weapons.
Isotope
A version of an element. Lithium-6 and lithium-7 are two isotopes of lithium.
Sanctions list
A government list of companies people in that country are not allowed to do business with.
UFLPA
A U.S. law that blocks imports made with forced labor in Xinjiang, a region of China.
In short

Three things we found

  1. The step fusion needs most, sorting out the rare lithium-6, has only been done on a large scale inside nuclear weapons programs. They often use toxic mercury.
  2. Shipping records show a U.S. company buying isotopes or lithium chemicals from five plants run by Russia's state nuclear agency.
  3. No fusion company has said where its lithium comes from, so nobody can check whether it was mined and made fairly.
