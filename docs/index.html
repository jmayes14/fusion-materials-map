Follow the lithium that will fuel fusion

Fusion power plants will make their own fuel from a rare kind of lithium. This story follows it from the ground to the power plant, and shows who could get hurt along the way.

1Step 1 of 4 · Dig

Salt flats of Chile and Argentina

Lithium-rich salt water is pumped from under the desert and left to dry in huge ponds.

6–18 mofor each batch of ponds to dry

Who could get hurt: Indigenous communities, who say they weren't asked and that their water is drying up.

On the map: the two glowing dots are the Atacama salt flat (Chile) and the Jujuy salt flats (Argentina).

Sources: Wilson Center; IISD; FIDH
1Step 1 of 4 · Dig

The Bikita mine, Zimbabwe

Here lithium comes from rock. Shipping records show this mine sending goods to a Chinese company in Hainan.

U.S. listZimbabwe's lithium is listed as made with child labor

Who could get hurt: children and mine workers, and villagers who report being moved off their land.

On the map: the arrow shows shipments from Bikita to China. The trail goes cold after that.

Sources: U.S. Department of Labor; Mongabay; Sayari
2Step 2 of 4 · Process

China cleans most of the world's lithium

Raw lithium is turned into clean chemicals that batteries and fusion projects can use.

70%of the world's lithium chemicals are made in China

Who could get hurt: Uyghurs and other minority groups in Xinjiang, where the U.S. says forced labor is a risk.

On the map: China, the Xinjiang region in the northwest, and the Hainan company that buys from Bikita.

Sources: IEA; U.S. Forced Labor Enforcement Task Force
3Step 3 of 4 · Sort

Russia's nuclear plants sell to a U.S. company

Sorting out the rare lithium-6 has only been done at scale inside nuclear weapons programs. Shipping records show five Russian state nuclear plants selling isotopes or lithium chemicals to one U.S. company.

5 plantsin Russia's nuclear system, one U.S. buyer

Who could get hurt: neighbors of secret nuclear sites, and everyone, if fusion money ends up supporting weapons programs.

On the map: the arrows run from the Russian plants to Isoflex USA in California. The five plants are NCCP (Novosibirsk), the Electrochemical Plant (Zelenogorsk), the Siberian Chemical Combine (Seversk), Elektrokhimpribor (Lesnoy), and Isotope JSC (Moscow).

Sources: Sayari; World Nuclear Association
3Step 3 of 4 · Sort

Oak Ridge, Tennessee: the last time the U.S. did it

In the 1950s, the U.S. sorted lithium for nuclear weapons here, using toxic mercury.

330 tonnesof mercury leaked into the land and a creek

Who could get hurt: people who live and work near Oak Ridge. The cleanup still isn't finished.

On the map: the Y-12 plant in Oak Ridge, Tennessee.

Sources: ATSDR (CDC); U.S. Department of Energy
4Step 4 of 4 · Use

The fusion projects that will need it

Lithium-6 is made into parts that wrap around the fusion reaction and make fuel. Test projects are running now in the U.S., the U.K., and France.

0fusion companies have said where their lithium comes from

Why it matters: without that, nobody can check whether the lithium was mined and made fairly.

On the map: Commonwealth Fusion Systems (Massachusetts), the U.K. fusion fuel program, and ITER (France).

Sources: ITER; UKAEA; Sayari
