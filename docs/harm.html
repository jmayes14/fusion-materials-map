Human rights

Who could get hurt, step by step

The last column says who reported the problem: a government, journalists or advocacy groups, or a risk that comes with the work itself. None of this is about a specific shipment of fusion lithium, because no fusion company has said where its lithium comes from.

StepWho is at riskWhat can happenWho reported it
Digging (Chile, Argentina)Indigenous communitiesWater drying up; mining approved without asking communities first; protests in 2023Journalists and advocacy groups
Digging (Zimbabwe)Children, mine workers, nearby villagesChild labor; families moved off their land; spills; unsafe workU.S. government
Digging (Australia)People living in the town of GreenbushesMine dust above legal limits, which a state report called a health riskState government
Processing (China)Uyghurs and other minority groups in XinjiangForced labor through government work programsU.S. government
Sorting (past, U.S.)People living and working near Oak Ridge, TennesseeHundreds of tonnes of toxic mercury leaked; cleanup still isn't finishedU.S. government
Sorting (now, Russia and China)Workers and neighbors of secret nuclear sites; everyone, if it helps build weaponsMercury pollution with no public oversight; money flowing to weapons programsShipping records
Making parts, running plants, cleanupPlant workersContact with tritium and toxic beryllium; nuclear wasteComes with the work
